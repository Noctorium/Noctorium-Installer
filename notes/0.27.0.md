Noctorium Song Find: which song is playing, from the microphone or from what your computer or phone is playing —
and every Noctorium is version 0.27.0.

## New

**Noctorium Song Find.** A new app of its own, like Shazam, for Windows, macOS, Linux and Android. Press the big
button and it listens, then tells you the song, the artist, the album and the year, with the cover, and links it on
Spotify, YouTube Music, Apple Music, Deezer and SoundCloud, and to its lyrics.

- **Two ways to listen.** *Microphone*, for a song playing in the room. *System audio* (on the phone, *Phone
  audio*), for what the device itself is playing — a video, a stream, a game, another app — with nothing held up to
  a speaker. On Windows that is the system's own loopback; on a Mac it needs macOS 14.2 or later and asks for
  permission once; on Linux it listens to the PulseAudio or PipeWire output; on Android 10 and later it asks
  Android first, as screen recording does, though only sound is recorded.
- **Every provider there is to ask.** Shazam, with nothing to set up. AudD, a few times a day without a token, or
  as often as you like with one. ACRCloud, with a free project's keys. On the desktop, AcoustID with MusicBrainz too,
  which is best with whole songs. Put them in the order you like in Settings › Providers, and have Song Find stop at
  the first that knows the song or ask every one and show what each said.
- **A history that stays home.** Every song found is kept on the device, searchable, each one removable, and
  cleared with one button. It is sent nowhere; Settings can turn it off.
- **Audio files, on the desktop.** Drop an MP3, FLAC, WAV, Ogg or M4A onto the window and it says which song it
  is. From a terminal, `noctorium-song-find --identify song.mp3`, or `--listen system`.
- **It updates itself,** as the rest of Noctorium does: on Windows it installs the new version by itself, checked
  against the release's checksum, ready the next time it starts; on the phone, Install hands the checked APK to
  Android's installer.

**Every installer offers it.** The window installer has a fifth box, the terminal installer takes
`--product song-find` and a sixth answer in its menu, `all` means all five, and the one-line scripts know it too:

```powershell
& ([scriptblock]::Create((irm https://noctorium.vercel.app/install))) --product song-find --yes
```

```bash
curl -fsSL https://noctorium.vercel.app/install | sh -s -- --product song-find --yes
```

The phone installer has a Noctorium Song Find button beside Noctorium Stats'.

**Obtainium.** Noctorium, Noctorium Stats and Noctorium Song Find can all be installed and kept up to date on
Android with [Obtainium](https://github.com/ImranR98/Obtainium). The website and Noctorium's GitHub page have an
*Get it on Obtainium* button for each, which opens Obtainium with the app set up. By hand: the source
`https://github.com/Noctorium/Noctorium-Installer`, and *Filter APKs by regular expression*
`^Noctorium-[0-9].*[.]apk$`, `^Noctorium-Stats-[0-9].*[.]apk$` or `^Noctorium-Song-Find-[0-9].*[.]apk$`.

## Worth knowing

- Shazam is asked the way its own apps ask, with only a fingerprint of the sound sent. It is not an official,
  documented service, so it could change without warning — the other providers are there for that day.
- Some Android apps forbid other apps from recording their sound; those cannot be heard with Phone audio. Use the
  microphone for them.
- Every Noctorium — the desktop, the phone, the CLI, Noctorium Stats on both, Noctorium Rust, Noctorium Song Find on
  both, the web player, the website, the account service and the installers — is numbered 0.27.0.
