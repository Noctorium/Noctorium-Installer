A volume booster to 1000%, the Toolbar and the Playlist layout, icon sets, Settings you can search, and shortcuts of your own.

## New

**A volume booster, up to 1000%.** The boost button under the volume now opens a slider from 100% to 1000%, ten times
as loud, always behind a limiter so a quiet song gets loud rather than broken. The volume button wears the boost —
"×4" — whenever it is on, so it is never forgotten, and past 200% it says to mind your ears and your speakers. On the
computer, the phone, in a terminal and in the browser. Songs Spotify plays in its own app, and YouTube in the
browser player's embedded YouTube player, are out of its reach, and it says so.

**The Toolbar, the Slider and the Playlist layout** — a desktop music player of the old school, as a choice beside the
others:

- *Toolbar*, a player bar of flat buttons in one row: add to a playlist, previous, play, pause, **stop**, next, the
  seek bar with "0:58 / 2:31", repeat, shuffle and the volume. At the top of the window or the foot.
- *Slider*, a seek bar of a thin groove and a small round handle.
- *Playlist*, a now playing layout: the cover docked in a panel with the song's details, the queue as a **table**
  under its tabs with the columns you choose and order (#, title, artist, album, length, service), an info bar with
  the song and a **visualiser**, and a **status bar** with the stream — "Opus, stereo, 48 kHz, 128 kbit/s" — and how
  far you are into the whole queue.
- The **visualiser**, bars or a level meter, is drawn from the sound itself, never a pattern pretending to be one,
  and measures nothing while it is not on screen.
- **Stop**, and **Stop after this song**, on the Toolbar, in the queue and in a song's menu; Stop can be added to the
  other player bars too.

On every Noctorium, and in Windows 98, Noctorium 98 and XP in their own style: a 98 toolbar, 98 column headings, a
status bar of sunken panes.

**Icon sets.** Standard, as it has always been, or Outlined, Rounded, Sharp, Two-tone, or Line — thin strokes in one
colour, after Lucide's. In Settings, under Look. In a terminal, its own symbols: Standard, plain ASCII, or Nerd Font.

**Settings, in order, with a search box.** Settings are now grouped — You, Look, Listening, Services, This device,
About — and the long pages are split, Customization into Theme and colours, Typeface and text, Player bar, Now
playing, and Home and browsing. Type in the box at the top to find any setting by its name, by what it does, or by
what you would call it — "dark mode", "louder", "hotkeys", "eq" — and it opens the page and lights the setting up.

**Shortcuts of your own, on the desktop.** Every shortcut is listed in Settings › Shortcuts and can be changed: press
the new keys, and Noctorium says if another action already has them. And **global shortcuts**, which work from any
program with Noctorium in the tray — off until you switch them on, registered the way each system means hot keys to
be, never by watching the keyboard.

**Quick search, Ctrl+F (⌘F on a Mac).** A search bar over everything: type, and the results come as you type; ↑ and ↓
to choose, Enter to play, Shift+Enter to add to the queue. Start or end with a **bang** to search one service:
`!ytm daft punk`, `!sc`, `!sp`, `!bc`, `!vk`, or `!all`. A global shortcut of its own brings the same bar up from
any program. Bangs work in every search box, on every Noctorium.

**The queue and Home.**

- **Clear all but this song**, beside Clear: the queue down to the song playing, and autoplay carries on after it.
- **A song picked on Home starts autoplay** from it, instead of queueing the rest of its shelf. The old way is a
  choice in Settings › Playback and queue.
- On the desktop, **right-click the song in the player bar**, in any layout, for its menu: Add to playlist, Stop
  after this song and the rest.

## Fixed

- In a terminal, the playback speed setting never reached the player; it now applies.
- On the phone, clearing the queue no longer forgets that the sound was muted.
