Keep Noctorium alive, on the phone: for phones that close apps in the background, Noctorium stays running, and comes
back by itself — mid-song, where it was — when it is stopped anyway.

## New

**Keep Noctorium alive**, under Settings → This phone. Off until you turn it on. Then:

- **It stays running in the background**, playing or paused, under the music's own notification; with nothing loaded,
  under a quiet one called "Keep Noctorium alive", which Android's settings can hide on its own. Noctorium Connect
  stays reachable all the while.
- **It comes back by itself** when Android or the phone's power manager stops it anyway, and carries on with the song it
  was stopped in the middle of. On a Xiaomi, killed mid-song, it was playing again from the same place six or seven
  seconds later. Stopped while paused, it comes back without playing.
- **It starts again by itself** after the phone restarts and after Noctorium is updated.
- **It says what happened**: how many times Android stopped Noctorium in the background in the last week, the last time
  and why, and whether Android refused to let it start again.
- **What this phone needs**, as a checklist with a button for each: Android's battery optimisation, notifications, and
  the phone maker's own autostart and battery pages — Xiaomi, Samsung, Huawei and Honor, OnePlus, Oppo and realme,
  Vivo and iQOO, Tecno and Infinix, Asus, Nokia, Meizu, Motorola, Lenovo and others — with that maker's page on
  dontkillmyapp.com for the rest.
- On a phone known to do this, or where Noctorium was stopped lately, Settings suggests it once.

## Better

- The Battery settings button opens the phone maker's own battery page on Samsung, Nokia and Lenovo, rather than
  Noctorium's app info.

## Worth knowing

- On a Xiaomi both Autostart and Android's battery optimisation must be allowed for Noctorium to come back: without
  Autostart nothing brings it back, and without the other Android lets it start but not play. The checklist marks both
  as needed there.
- A force stop — from Android's app settings, or a phone maker's clean-up that force-stops — keeps any app off until it
  is opened again. Nothing can get round that; keep-alive comes back the next time Noctorium opens.
