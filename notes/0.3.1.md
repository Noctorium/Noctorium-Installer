A spinner that never stopped, and thirty-one ways to lose a setting.

## The play button could get stuck, and there was no way out

Pressing play sometimes left a spinner where the play button should be, forever. Restarting Spiceity was the only escape.

The spinner is drawn for one status — resolving — and the button was **disabled** for exactly as long as it showed. So anything that left the resolving step without saying what happened took away the one control that could have recovered it.

Three separate things could do that, and all three are fixed:

- A failure of a kind the error handling did not name went straight past it. Nothing was logged, no message was shown, and the status never moved.
- Pressing a different track while one was still resolving was reported back as a *playback failure*, as though something had gone wrong.
- Two tracks starting at once — a double click, or the queue advancing under one — could have one player shut down by the other's cleanup.

Underneath those, the player now guarantees it never ends a play attempt still resolving, whatever happened on the way. **And the spinner is now a stop button:** press it to give up on a track instead of waiting.

## Settings that quietly undid themselves

Thirty-one places read the whole playback state, changed one field, and wrote it all back — losing anything written in between. The player's progress clock writes four times a second, so this was easy to hit:

- Pausing at the wrong moment flipped straight back to playing
- Changing the volume could undo a mute
- When a track stopped, the one before it could reappear

The queue had a worse version: skipping while a playlist was starting could bring the **entire previous track list** back. Skipping now decides against the queue as it is at the moment you skip.

## Also

The playback log used to swallow its own write failures, so a missing entry looked like an event that never happened. It now says so, once, when it cannot write.

Download it from this page. Spiceity's own updater cannot see this release: it asks GitHub's `/releases/latest`, which answers 404 for a private repository, and Spiceity reads that as being up to date.
