The volume boost works, and the phone has one too.

## The boost was making things quieter, not louder

On the desktop it set mpv's volume to 200%, and the slider stretched to ten times unity. mpv's volume
curve is cubic, so 200 is not twice as loud — it is eight times the amplitude, and against music
mastered anywhere near full scale that is not headroom, it is clipping.

Rendered to a file and measured: a tone at volume 200 peaked at exactly 100% of full scale, and at
volume 400 it *still* peaked at exactly 100%. Turning it up further changed nothing but the distortion.
Which is precisely what it looked like from the outside — a button that appeared to do nothing.

Louder means lifting the quiet parts, not shoving the loud ones through the ceiling. The boost is now a
compressor with make-up gain and a limiter behind it. Same tone, same measurement: the peak held where
it was, and RMS — which is what loudness actually follows — went from 8.8% of full scale to 63.8%.

The volume stops at 100% either way now. The boost no longer moves the slider, so the number under it
means one thing, and there is no longer a way to drag it into distortion.

## Volume on the phone

Now playing gets a mute button, a slider, and the same Boost.

The phone has volume keys already, which is why this took a while to arrive — but they set the stream.
They cannot put Noctorium quieter than everything else, so turning a podcast down turns the next
notification down with it, and they cannot reach a boost at all.

The boost there is Android's own loudness enhancer on the player's audio session, nine decibels, and it
compresses rather than scales for the same reason the desktop's does. If the platform refuses it — some
devices will — the chip stays off rather than claiming a boost that is not there.

Nothing else has changed since 0.4.6.
