Windows 98 and Windows XP as themselves, five more seek bars, and more ways to lay out the player bar and Now playing.

## New

**Windows 98 and XP, the whole look.** The two Windows themes were a grey palette and a beige one; now they are
those desktops.

- *98*: grey bevelled buttons and boxes, navy title bars, a toolbar down the side, pages in a white sunken pane
  with scroll bars that have arrows, menus and dialogs as 98 drew them (the × closes them), Settings as a
  Control Panel of group boxes, checkboxes and option buttons, and Now playing as windows on the teal desktop.
- *XP*: Luna's blue title bars and rounded buttons, Explorer's task pane down the side, the blue taskbar with its
  green start button, and Now playing as windows over a blue sky and a green hill.

On the desktop they use the period's type, MS Sans Serif and Tahoma, where the computer has it. On the phone the
tabs become a taskbar with a start button and a clock; in the terminal the title bars run from navy to blue and
the windows are bevelled in block characters; in the browser it is the desktop, start menu and all. Only
Noctorium's own name and mark appear.

**The taskbar's clock** can be put away: *Show the clock* in Settings, or right-click the taskbar on the desktop
and in the browser, or hold the clock on the phone -- as Windows let you.

**Five more seek bars**, on every Noctorium:

- *Bars*: a row of bars like SoundCloud's, lit as the song plays. Every song has a row of its own: a pattern it
  keeps, not its waveform, since Noctorium never holds the audio to measure.
- *Beads*: a string of dots.
- *Neon*: a glowing line with a spark at its head.
- *Ruler*: a tick every few seconds and a longer one each minute, with a pointer riding above.
- *Luna*: Windows XP's green blocks, beside 98's *Classic*.

**More player bars.**

- On the desktop: *Floating*, lifted off the window's edge like a dock; *Island*, a small pill that opens to the
  seek bar and the volume when pointed at; *Display*, the song in a display in the middle, like a stereo's; and
  *Taskbar*: a start button that opens Now playing, the song as a pressed button, and a tray with the volume and
  the clock.
- On the phone: *Floating*, *Line*, *Record* (the cover as a small record that turns) and *Taskbar*.
- In the terminal: *Compact*, one row, and *Taskbar*.

Settings shows each layout as a picture.

**More Now playing layouts.**

- On the desktop: *Full cover*; *Split*; *Cover flow*, the queue's covers turned towards the one playing -- click
  one to play it; *Turntable*, the record on a deck with its arm crossing as the song plays; and *Big type*.
- On the phone, a choice for the first time: *Classic* as it was, *Full cover*, *Record*, *Cover flow* (swipe
  through the queue), *Sing along* and *Big type*.
- In the terminal: *Big type* in block letters, *Cover* and *Lyrics*.

## Fixed

- A Spotify song coming up next that could not be matched said so in a banner before anybody had asked to play
  it. The look ahead is quiet now; pressing play still looks again and explains.
- The Inline player bar gives up its extras in turn as the window narrows, rather than squeezing the seek bar to
  nothing and pushing the volume off the end.
- In the browser, the songs beside Search's top result have room for their titles.
- A 256-colour terminal shows each colour as the nearest one it has. XP's beige came out pink.
- The terminal player keeps its other choices when it meets one from a newer version.
- The desktop's seek bars no longer redraw sixty times a second when nothing in them moves.

## Worth knowing

- Windows 11 still draws the window's own title bar; the 98 and XP themes only colour it.
- The skins use the fonts the computer already has; on Linux, which seldom has them, they fall back to the usual
  one.
- Under the Windows themes the phone leaves glass and the corner choice aside, and says so.
