Noctorium on the Mac.

## New

**macOS.** Noctorium for Apple silicon and for Intel Macs, as a disk image: open it and drag Noctorium into
Applications. It is the same Noctorium as on Windows and Linux — both libraries, the queue, likes and
playlists on your own accounts, synced lyrics, every theme — and it carries everything it needs, mpv and
yt-dlp included, so nothing has to be installed first.

- On a Mac it lives in the menu bar when you close its window, comes back when you click it in the Dock,
  and Command-Q stops the music before it quits.
- Your sign-ins are kept in the macOS Keychain, and Noctorium's own things in
  ~/Library/Application Support/Noctorium.
- Start with your Mac, in its window or straight into the menu bar, from Settings.
- It updates itself, as on the other systems: the new version replaces the old one in place and starts.

**In one line on a Mac too.** In Terminal:

```bash
curl -fsSL https://noctorium.vercel.app/install | sh
```

It picks the right disk image for your Mac — asking the hardware, so Apple silicon gets Apple silicon even
under Rosetta — checks it against the published checksums and puts Noctorium in Applications. It installs the
Noctorium CLI as well if you ask, or instead.

**The Noctorium CLI on macOS**, for Apple silicon and Intel: the player in a terminal, and `noctorium web`.
It uses the mpv inside Noctorium when that is installed, and fetches its own otherwise.

## Worth knowing

- Noctorium is not signed with an Apple developer identity, so a disk image downloaded in a browser is
  asked about once: if macOS says it cannot check Noctorium, open System Settings, Privacy & Security, and
  choose Open Anyway. Installed with the one line above, it is not asked about at all.
- Every Mac build is installed, tested and opened on a Mac before it is released: the bundled mpv and yt-dlp
  have to start, the Keychain has to keep a secret, the sign-in browser has to load a page, and the window
  has to open and quit cleanly.
