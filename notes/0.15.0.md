Noctorium Connect, as a tab of its own with the player as its remote, and a volume booster that gets louder.

## New

**Noctorium Connect has a tab of its own**, in the sidebar on the computer and the tabs along the foot on the phone.

- **Where the music is**, big at the top: the cover, the song, how far in, and what this device has to do with it —
  playing it, or the remote control for the device that is.
- **Every device on your network**, each with what it is playing or "Idle", whether it is a computer, a phone or a
  terminal, and one button: **Play here**, or **Play on** it. The music moves at the same second, with the whole
  queue, from wherever it is — even between two devices you are not holding.
- **Who is controlling whom**, and, when nothing is found, what to check: the same account, the same Wi-Fi, the
  computer's firewall, and the phone allowed to play in the background. A device can also be added by its address.

**The player is the remote.** While the music is on another device, the player bar and Now playing here show that
device's song, cover and position, and their buttons drive it: play and pause, next and previous, the seek bar, the
volume, shuffle and repeat, and the queue. A strip on the player bar says **Playing on Desk** — or **Controlled from
Phone** on the device being driven — and opens the tab. The small Connect window is gone. On the phone, a headset's or
the lock screen's buttons drive the device that is playing, and nothing starts here by accident.

**Every device in step.** Each device says what it is playing, and who it is driving, as soon as either changes, so
every Connect tab shows the same thing within about a second. A device let go of stops saying it is controlled within
moments.

## Fixed

**The volume booster gets louder.** It was switched on and barely changed anything on the computer, and on the phone it
made loud music quieter. Its limiter, meant only to stop the sound breaking up, was holding back nearly all the boost,
and Android's loudness effect turned out to compress loud music down. Now the booster uses the room your volume leaves
first, then limits with a look-ahead limiter and a gentle clipper, on the computer, the phone and in the browser alike.
Each step is clearly louder: music already mastered loud gains about 3, 5 and 6 dB at 200%, 400% and 1000%, and up to
14 dB from the computer's usual volume; quiet recordings get close to the full 20 dB. The top levels sound dense — the
only way to make loud music louder still — and the "mind your ears" warning stands.

- On the computer, a click on the booster's slider sets the level it shows; it sometimes left the old one playing.
- After a song in Spotify's own app, the booster is offered again as soon as a song plays here.

## Worth knowing

- Devices on 0.14.2 and earlier still connect, but show as "Idle" in the tab, since they do not say what they play.
  Update them all for the full picture.
- On Windows, when the firewall asks about Noctorium, allow it on the network you are on — tick Public too if Windows
  calls your home network Public.
